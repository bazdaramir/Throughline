## Goal

Find out whether the trigger written into [[D-0002 Store sessions in Redis with a sliding TTL]] has actually fired, and if so, decide.

## What happened

- Pulled the July dashboard-query fix and re-ran the session-lookup benchmark against Postgres 16 in staging: p99 3.1ms, budget 5ms. The trigger has fired.
- Confirmed both Redis incidents ([[G-0001 Redis TTL silently resets on SET]], and the logout race) are structural to running two stores, not implementation defects.
- Wrote a throwaway Postgres adapter to get a real number rather than a guessed one. Not committed.
- Recorded [[D-0004 Move session storage to Postgres]] with `confidence: medium`, because the benchmark is synthetic.

## What changed

- `src/auth/store/postgres.ts` — new, scratch quality, kept only to reproduce the benchmark.
- `bench/session-lookup.ts` — new benchmark harness.
- Nothing in `src/auth/session.ts`. The interface was deliberately left alone.

## Dead ends

- **Spent 40 minutes trying to fix the TTL reset inside Redis** — a Lua script doing SET+EXPIRE atomically. It works, and it is the wrong altitude: it fixes one of two symptoms of having two stores, and leaves the revocation race untouched. Abandoned. **Do not restart this.**
- **Considered a read-through cache in front of Postgres** to get both latency and consistency. Retains the revocation race and adds invalidation on top — strictly worse than either endpoint. Recorded in the alternatives table of [[D-0004 Move session storage to Postgres]] so it does not get re-proposed.
- Benchmarked with a uniform random key distribution before realising real traffic is heavily skewed toward recently-issued sessions. The 3.1ms number is therefore probably *pessimistic*, but it is not trustworthy either way.
